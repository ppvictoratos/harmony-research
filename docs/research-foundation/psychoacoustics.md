# Psychoacoustics & Music Therapy for Parkinson's

**Reference:** [Psychoacoustics - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychoacoustics)

Psychoacoustics is the study of sound perception—how the human auditory system and brain process, interpret, and respond to acoustic stimuli. Understanding psychoacoustics is central to this research because it explains the *mechanism* by which music properties (rhythm, tempo, pitch, timbre) influence motor control, cognition, and mood in Parkinson's disease.

---

## Core Psychoacoustic Concepts

### 1. **Temporal Processing & Rhythm**
- The brain has an intrinsic timing system that synchronizes to external rhythmic stimuli
- **Relevance:** Rhythmic music can entrain (synchronize) motor timing in Parkinson's patients, helping overcome bradykinesia (slow movement) and freezing of gait
- **Evidence base:** Rhythmic Auditory Cueing (RAC) is well-established in Parkinson's literature

### 2. **Frequency Perception & Pitch**
- The cochlea (inner ear) performs spectral analysis, decomposing complex sounds into frequency components
- **Relevance:** Different frequencies may engage different neural pathways; major vs. minor tonality may evoke different emotional/physiological responses
- **Research gap:** Which frequencies/chord progressions optimize motor vs. emotional outcomes?

### 3. **Loudness & Dynamic Range**
- Loudness perception is nonlinear and frequency-dependent
- **Relevance:** Sound pressure levels and dynamic range may affect attention, arousal, and motor engagement
- **Research gap:** Optimal loudness range for therapeutic sessions?

### 4. **Timbre & Spectral Complexity**
- Timbre is the perceptual quality allowing us to distinguish instruments/sounds with the same pitch and loudness
- **Relevance:** Different timbres (e.g., piano vs. violin vs. synthesizer) may engage auditory and motor systems differently
- **Research gap:** Does instrument selection matter for therapeutic outcomes?

### 5. **Auditory Stream Segregation**
- The brain groups sound elements into "streams" (e.g., melody vs. accompaniment in a musical piece)
- **Relevance:** Complex polyphonic music may create multiple attentional streams; simple rhythmic patterns may be easier for movement synchronization
- **Research gap:** Simple vs. complex music for motor control?

## Psychoacoustics → Music Therapy Mechanism

```
Acoustic Stimulus (rhythm, pitch, tempo, timbre)
        ↓
Cochlear Analysis (frequency decomposition)
        ↓
Auditory Processing (brainstem, midbrain, cortex)
        ↓
Motor System Engagement (premotor cortex, basal ganglia, cerebellum)
        ↓
Behavioral Outcome (gait improvement, tremor reduction, mood elevation)
```

**Key insight:** Music bypasses some of the damaged basal ganglia circuits in Parkinson's by providing external temporal cues (rhythmic entrainment) that the motor system can follow.

## Research Questions This Informs

### Motor Control
1. **Rhythm & Gait:** Which tempo ranges (BPM) best entrain gait? Is there an optimal range? Does it vary by patient?
2. **Timbre & Fine Motor:** Do certain instruments/timbres improve fine motor control better than others?
3. **Tonality & Movement:** Does major vs. minor key affect motor fluency or emotional state during movement?
4. **Polyrhythm & Complexity:** Can patients with Parkinson's synchronize to polyrhythmic patterns, or does complexity impair performance?

### Cognitive & Emotional
1. **Pitch Perception:** Are higher vs. lower frequencies more effective for attention/engagement?
2. **Dynamic Range:** What loudness levels optimize listening without causing stress?
3. **Familiarity:** Does familiar music vs. novel music affect synchronization and emotional response?

### Neurological Mechanisms
1. **Auditory-Motor Coupling:** What brain regions mediate rhythm-driven motor improvement? (fMRI/EEG research)
2. **Dopamine & Music Reward:** Does rhythm/timbre activate dopaminergic reward pathways?
3. **Cerebellar Timing:** Can music engage cerebellar timing networks to compensate for basal ganglia dysfunction?

## Evidence Base to Gather

When aggregating research, look for studies that:

- **Report specific psychoacoustic parameters:** tempo (BPM), key/tonality, timbre, complexity
- **Measure motor outcomes:** gait speed, cadence, stride length, fine motor dexterity, tremor, freezing episodes
- **Measure cognitive/emotional outcomes:** mood, attention, anxiety, engagement
- **Use matched controls:** comparing different music properties head-to-head (e.g., 100 BPM vs. 120 BPM)
- **Report mechanism:** neuroimaging, electrophysiology, or physiological markers (EMG, heart rate)

## Psychoacoustics in Phase 2 (Gap Finder)

When building the gap finder (Phase 2), use psychoacoustic theory to generate hypotheses:

**Example gap discovery:**
- ✓ Many studies: "Rhythmic music + PT improves gait"
- ✗ Few studies: "Polyrhythmic (complex rhythm) + PT for fine motor control"
- ✗ Few studies: "Minor vs. major chords for mood + gait coupling"
- ✗ Few studies: "Timbre (piano vs. electronic) effect on tremor reduction"

The gap finder should score unexplored combinations using both:
1. **Publication frequency** (what hasn't been tested?)
2. **Psychoacoustic plausibility** (what *should* be tested based on sound-perception theory?)
